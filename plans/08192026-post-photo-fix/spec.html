Never Crop User Photos

kylie/post-photo-fix
Merge target
dev
Size
Large · 6 tasks · 17 app files
Risk
Level 3 · structural risk

Why

A portrait post is visibly cut off because the frontend forces uploaded images into fixed frames with object-cover. The upload pipeline stores the original image unchanged; every lost edge is a presentation decision. The requirement is now explicit: no existing or future user-uploaded image may be visibly cropped anywhere in the app.

What

Adopt one no-crop rendering policy across regular post photos, shared-post previews, comment attachments, message media, composer thumbnails, profile covers, and profile-photo dialogs. Full post and photo-comment media use their natural height directly—no fixed frame or neutral stage around them. Tall or wide images scale down to fit. Multi-photo post previews retain the established fixed two-by-two carousel grid and +N counter; this compact preview is the explicit crop-to-fill exception. Clicking a post photo or carousel tile first opens the post album; from that album, clicking an individual photo opens a full-image viewer for the selected photo. Clicking a profile-header avatar opens its existing enlarged circular profile-picture view; profile owners retain a separate camera control for change, view, and remove actions. Identity avatars and profile-picture activity remain intentionally circular, centered crops without an added square backdrop.

Done when: full post content images show every edge with no extra surrounding panel; multi-photo posts show the original four-tile carousel with its remaining-photo counter; clicking through to an album and then a photo opens the selected image in a dismissible viewer; clicking a profile-header avatar opens its close-up; identity avatars remain circular; and constraints remain responsive and usable.

Grill decisions locked 2026-08-19

Coverage

All content photos. This includes posts, comments, messages, shares, composer previews, and covers. Identity avatars and profile-picture activity stay circular so the app keeps its recognizable profile treatment.

Oversize policy

Fit, never cut. Full media is capped at a viewport-safe height (target: 70dvh) and scales down with all pixels visible.

Constrained shapes

Preserve the compact carousel grid. Multi-photo post previews retain the established fixed footprint and crop-to-fill behavior with a +N counter. Small thumbnails and cover banners contain the full image instead of filling by crop.

Required pushback — Level 3: this reverses a global visual convention across 17 files. Circular avatars and banner covers will show smaller photos with neutral space rather than close-up crops. The user explicitly restored crop-to-fill in the compact multi-photo carousel preview to keep its four-tile layout and +N counter; opened post photos remain no-crop.

Context and policy

Root cause: object-cover, fixed aspect-ratio frames, absolute image fill, and clipping max-heights across the frontend. The API and upload middleware retain original files and require no changes.

Blast radius and constraints

Must

  • Apply the no-crop policy to every user-visible uploaded image surface.
  • Ensure existing and future uploads follow the same policy automatically.
  • Keep all image links, dialogs, counters, actions, uploads, and responsive layouts working.
  • Use existing neutral colors/tokens; no new dependency or image-processing service.

Must not

  • No backend, API, schema, migration, storage, or upload-file mutation.
  • No client-side crop, resize-and-reupload, EXIF transform, or image replacement for content photos.
  • No silent exceptions for grids, previews, covers, comments, or messages; circular identity avatars are the explicit exception.
  • No unrelated feed, chat, profile, or theme redesign.

Out of scope

  • New zoom, pan, crop-editor, or slideshow features.
  • Changing the underlying image files or their original dimensions.
  • Non-user decorative assets, icons, skeletons, gradients, and chat-theme swatches.
